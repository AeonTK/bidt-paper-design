Deploying agentic AI in regulated contexts requires navigating two interrelated design dimensions: \emph{autonomy}---how much human involvement a workflow requires---and \emph{agency}---what the system can perceive and affect. Though often treated independently, the two are coupled: at higher autonomy, human error correction is less available, so reliable operation requires appropriately constraining agency. Compliance requirements reinforce this coupling by mandating human involvement as the consequences of actions grow. Yet no established approach addresses them jointly as a design problem, leaving practitioners without a principled basis for reasoning about where oversight should sit, what consequences agent actions can have, and how errors can be corrected.

We introduce a two-dimensional design space in which both dimensions are organised into five operational levels, and propose six architectural tactics---checkpoints, escalation, multi-agent delegation, tool provisioning, tool fencing, and write staging---for navigating it. The tactics are demonstrated through a worked example of an automated funding program matching tool to support citizen-driven innovation. The design space and tactics provide a shared vocabulary for principled, compliance-aware agentic AI design in which responsibility, auditability, and reversibility are explicit design considerations.
