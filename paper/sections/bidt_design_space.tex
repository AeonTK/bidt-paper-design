\section{Autonomy and Agency as Design Dimensions}

The coupling has both practical and regulatory roots. Practically, an agent operating at higher autonomy has less opportunity for human error correction, so reliable task completion requires constraining agency to what the system can handle independently~\cite{Feng2025Levels,Mitchell2025}. Compliance requirements reinforce this further: the EU~AI~Act mandates human oversight for high-risk applications; the GDPR constrains data access and grants rights to explanation over automated decisions~\cite{eu_ai_act_2024,gdpr_2016}, defining a viability envelope within which deployments must remain. Each dimension is operationalised into five levels; Figure~\ref{fig:design_space} shows the resulting design space.

Following Feng et al.~\cite{Feng2025Levels}, we distinguish five autonomy levels that describe the \emph{interaction design}: how control, review, and responsibility are allocated between human and agent. At \emph{L1~(Operator)}, the human commands and the agent executes a specific sub-task; at \emph{L2~(Collaborator)}, both share a workspace and iterate. At \emph{L3~(Consultant)}, the agent leads planning but pauses to confirm direction; at \emph{L4~(Approver)}, it executes and pauses only for explicit approval before consequential actions. At \emph{L5~(Observer)}, the agent operates fully autonomously while the human monitors and can intervene.

The five agency levels, introduced in this work, provide a cumulative scale of increasing operational reach. \emph{L1~(Model-only)} covers reasoning and drafting from provided context with no tool use; \emph{L2~(Internal agent)} adds safe local tools with no external access; \emph{L3~(Read-only agent)} permits read-only access to external sources. \emph{L4~(Pragmatic, reversible)} allows external writes with low rollback cost; \emph{L5~(Pragmatic, commit)} covers writes with costly undo---final submissions and authoritative records.
